How to build a worksheet

Each worksheet is a printable page of small piano keyboards. You mark keys for the left and right hand, name the chord or scale, and add finger numbers or a grand staff if you want them. Leave things blank to turn the page into an assignment.

1. Start a sheet

On the home screen, pick a layout under Start a new sheet and give the sheet a name. The name is only for finding it in your list; it doesn't print.

  • 8 chords: 4 rows of 2 keyboards.
  • Two hands: 4 rows of 1 wide keyboard. Use this for scales and two-hand voicings.
  • 15 chords: 5 rows of 3 small keyboards.
  • Mixed: rows of 3, 2 and 1.

Tap the big title at the top of the page to change what prints there, such as "Major Scales". The Name and Date lines are for your student to fill in.

Back on the home screen, the ••• button next to a sheet lets you rename, duplicate, export or delete it.

2. Arrange the page

Tap Layout to change the page. Each row can hold 1, 2 or 3 keyboards: fewer keyboards in a row gives each one more keys. Use the arrows to move a row, ✕ to delete it and + Add row to add one. Tap Done with layout when you're finished.

To move a chord to another spot, drag it. On a phone or tablet, press and hold it first, then drag. The chords in between shift over by one.

+ Add page at the bottom adds a page with the same layout. In Layout you can also duplicate or delete a page.

3. Build a chord

Tap any keyboard on the page to open the editor. The ‹ and › arrows at the top move to the previous or next keyboard without closing it.

In the Builder, choose Chord, then tap in order:

  1. Root: a letter, then ♮, ♯ or ♭.
  2. Quality: maj, min, dim, aug, sus2, sus4 or 5.
  3. Seventh: none, 6, 7, maj7 or dim7.
  4. Optional: an extension (9, 11, 13), alterations (♭5, ♯9 and so on), add and omit, or a bass note for a slash chord like C/E.

Most chords take three taps: G, min, 7 gives Gm7. The chord's name and notes show under the buttons, and the keyboard marks a simple voicing for you, which you can change.

Faded buttons aren't allowed with what you've already picked. Tap one and the app says why.

Your own label instead

Type in Custom label to print any text instead of a built chord. Type ? to make a "name this chord" question. If what you type is a chord symbol, like Bb7, the app offers to turn it into a built chord.

4. Mark keys and fingers

  • Choose Left hand or Right hand, then tap keys to mark them. Tap a marked key again to clear it.
  • Choose Add finger numbers and a finger from 1 to 5, then tap keys. The numbers print under the keyboard. Choose Mark notes to go back to marking.
  • Use « ‹ › » to slide the keyboard by a key or an octave if notes don't fit. A yellow tag on the page means a marked note is out of view.
  • ♯ and ♭ pick how black keys are named when they aren't part of a built chord or scale.
  • Copy to next copies this keyboard to the next one so you can change it a little. Clear notes clears marks and fingers; Clear all clears everything.

5. Build a scale

Scales need the wide keyboard, so they're only available in rows with 1 keyboard (the Two hands layout, or set a row to 1 in Layout). In the Builder, choose Scale, then pick:

  • Tonic: a letter, then ♮, ♯ or ♭.
  • Type: major, natural minor, harmonic minor or melodic minor.
  • Octaves: 1 or 2.
  • Hands: right, left or both. Both hands play two octaves apart, one octave each.
  • Direction: up, or up and down.

The scale marks its keys and fills in the standard fingering. Red finger numbers are the starting finger and each place the hand crosses (thumb under or finger over). Tap a different finger on a key to change it. Your change replaces the standard finger and loses the red.

The fingerings follow Alfred's Complete Book of Scales. Melodic minor scales that start on a black key don't have fingering yet; tap those in yourself. You can check every fingering on the fingering page.

Keys with no key signature, like G♯ major, aren't offered. The app suggests the other spelling (A♭ major). If you pick a tonic that only exists in the other mode, it switches between major and minor and tells you.

6. Add a grand staff

In the editor, tap Show above the keyboard to put a grand staff between the name and the keyboard. Right-hand keys go on the treble staff and left-hand keys on the bass.

  • A chord shows as one stacked chord in quarter notes.
  • A scale shows note by note with its key signature and finger numbers, going up and back down if you chose that.

The staff takes room from the keyboard. On a page with a staff, 3 rows or fewer keep the keyboards near full size.

7. Transpose

  • The whole sheet: tap Transpose at the top, step up or down by half steps and check the preview. Each chord's keys move up or down, whichever stays closer to middle C.
  • As a copy: tick Make a transposed copy to keep the original and get a new sheet in the new key. This is the quick way to make the same worksheet in every key.
  • One keyboard: in the editor, use − ½ step and + ½ step.

8. Colors and chord symbols

Hands sets how marked keys look for each hand: a solid color, a pattern or a dot, with the note name if you like. If the two hands might look alike on a black-and-white printer, the app warns you. Pick one dark and one light color, or different styles.

Symbols switches chord names between Standard (Cmaj7, Cm7, Cdim) and Jazz (C∆7, C–7, C°), and between Plain spelling, which avoids double sharps and flats, and Strict.

9. Print an answer key and a student copy

Tap Print. Leave everything on for your answer key. Turn things off for a student copy:

  • Chord names off: students name the chords.
  • Marked notes off: students mark the keys (finger numbers hide too).
  • Notes on the grand staff off: students write the notes on blank staves.
  • Student name and date lines: on or off.

For the same result every time, set Scale to 100% (not "Fit to page") and turn off headers and footers in the print window. On iPhone and iPad the app already shrinks the page slightly so the last row prints correctly.

10. Worksheet ideas

Name the chord

Build chords, then print with Chord names off.

Mark the keys

Build chords, then print with Marked notes off.

Write it on the staff

Turn on the staff, then print with Notes on the grand staff off.

Scale practice

Two hands layout, one scale per row, staff on, fingering in.

Every key

Make one sheet, then Transpose as a copy for each key.

Mystery chord

Mark keys and type ? as the custom label.

11. Saving, backup and examples

Everything saves as you go, on this device only. Undo steps back through your changes to the open sheet.

  • Export all sheets on the home screen saves a backup file. Use it now and then, and to move sheets to another device with Import sheets.
  • On iPhone or iPad, use Share, then Add to Home Screen, to install the app. Open it once while online, and after that it works offline.
  • If the home screen shows Examples, tap one to open your own copy to use or change.
